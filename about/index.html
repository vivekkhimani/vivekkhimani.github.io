I got into computer science because I loved mathematics, and it felt like a good place to put it to work. I liked the point where an abstract idea stops being an idea and starts doing something in the real world. I spent a few years working on privacy-preserving machine learning, where that tension was especially clear. The data that could make a model most useful is often the data people have the strongest reason to keep private.

I first came across Semgrep while I was in college, when I used it to grade data structures assignments. I liked the product enough that I eventually joined the company. I got to work with the nicest people, start new products from scratch, and see them make their way into the hands of customers. It was refreshing to step outside of writing code and see how the rest of the company worked: how customers made decisions, how products were sold and supported, and how all the pieces fit together.

At some point, I've always wanted to start a business of my own. I grew up watching my dad run a small agricultural business. It was a good business, but the economics were straightforward: more people, more land, more resources, more output. I saw how much work could go into growing something when the outcome was still tied closely to the amount of time and resources you could put into it. Software is different. A good piece of software can be built once and used by thousands or millions of people. I like that leverage, and I'd love to eventually use it in a physical industry far from Silicon Valley, where better software can have a very tangible effect on how people work.

I've been an athlete for most of my life. Squash was the serious one growing up, and I still play, along with tennis and running. A lot of what I like about sport is the repetition. You break a movement or a concept down, practice the same thing over and over, pay attention to what is slightly off, and eventually it starts to feel natural. There isn't much room to hide from your own mistakes. You have to stay focused and keep going. I find myself approaching work in much the same way. Running, in particular, is still one of the easiest ways for me to clear my head.

I've also traveled quite a bit, sometimes for work and often on my own. I like being somewhere I don't know anyone and having to figure things out as I go. The best part is usually the people I meet along the way. Hearing how someone else ended up where they are, what they care about, and what their day-to-day life looks like is a good reminder that there are a lot of ways to live a life.

When I'm not working or playing a sport, I'm usually reading, cooking, playing guitar, or looking for something new to get into. I read mostly fiction and memoir, with a soft spot for murder mysteries and stories built around real lives. I've played guitar for some time now, and in general, I like having a few things in my life that don't need to lead anywhere.
