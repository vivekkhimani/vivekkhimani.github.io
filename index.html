Vivek Khimani

Software engineer at Semgrep · San Francisco Bay Area

I came to computer science through mathematics, and stayed for the places where the math has to survive contact with something real. For a while that meant privacy-preserving machine learning, which is close to a contradiction in terms and matters most exactly where it is hardest: health data, where the records that would teach a model the most are the ones nobody should be handing over.

I found Semgrep before I found the job. I was using it to grade data structures assignments, and it was the rare open-source developer tool I actually enjoyed. I joined pre-revenue and have watched it become a business, which has taught me more than anything else I have done. At some point I want to build something of my own. I think software does its most interesting work well away from Silicon Valley, in industries nobody has bothered to look at.

I have been an athlete my whole life. Squash was the serious one until college; now it is running, tennis and squash for the pleasure of it. Squash is also where I learned how I learn, which is stubbornly: take the thing apart, stay with it past the point it stops being fun, keep going until the root cause turns up. Running is where I think. Reading is where I go to be somewhere else, mostly fiction and memoir, with a standing weakness for murder mysteries and for anything grounded in a real life. I am still bad at the guitar and considerably better in a kitchen.
